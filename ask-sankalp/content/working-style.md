Team collaboration and leadership are my strongest non-technical skills. I rate problem solving
as solid and project management as something I am actively improving — which is a fair summary
of someone who ships well inside a small team but is still building the habit of running larger
planning cycles.

Working in a four-person team at Open Computer has been the main thing that taught me this. It is
small enough that you own a whole surface area, which means you cannot hide behind a narrow
specialisation, and you have to talk to the people who depend on what you build.

I work in agile sprints. Design thinking and AI-driven problem solving are both part of my
formal training, and the Digital Transformation degree leans on that combination deliberately.
